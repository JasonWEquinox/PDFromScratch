Steal And Upgrade

Tools people tolerate but hate (stale, abandoned, bloated, overpriced), then the solo-dev upgrade.

Club & team life 9

  • #01Grassroots club team app as a lean TeamSnap alternative: flat per-club price, no ads, built for NZ/AU club rhythms
  • #02Privacy-first alternative to free-but-data-hungry club apps (Spond-style): no data resale, simple flat fee, parents trust it
  • #06Referee/umpire availability and allocation for local associations — currently spreadsheets and phone trees
  • #07Volunteer duty roster for junior clubs (canteen, oranges, line marking, scoring) replacing ad-laden SignUpGenius-style sites
  • #08Kids-sport carpool / lift-share rota that rotates fairly and reminds parents
  • #09Fair-playing-time tracker for junior coaches: equal minutes, position rotation, season totals
  • #10Lineup and substitution planner for positional sports (netball, basketball, hockey) with printable quarter-by-quarter sheets
  • #11Club subs/fee tracker with bank-statement CSV reconciliation (NZ clubs pay by bank transfer, not cards)
  • #24Class booking for tiny fitness/yoga/martial-arts studios priced far below big gym platforms

Competitions 4

  • #03Fixture/draw generator for small competitions (round robin, venue + time-slot + bye constraints) replacing Excel macros and old desktop tools
  • #04Box/ladder ranking for squash, tennis, badminton clubs — many still run on ancient PHP sites or noticeboards
  • #05Social sport night-league manager for small operators (futsal, netball, touch, indoor cricket): teams, fixtures, results, standings
  • #12One-day tournament runner: pools, brackets, court/field allocation, live results via QR code on the day

Niche meets & games 6

  • #28Social golf group competitions: stableford, handicaps, season order-of-merit
  • #29Pub-quiz / quiz-night scoring and question-pack manager for hosts
  • #30Private fantasy-style league for workplace or club groups around local competitions

02Round 2 · the clubroom wall

Question Storming

Only questions, surfacing who pays and where the real pain is.

Who paysWhere it hurtsMarket & moatRunning it soloReachThe hook
  1. Q1Who has a budget but no IT person — club treasurers, league operators, association secretaries?
  2. Q2What do club volunteers already pay for monthly, out of pocket or from club funds?
  3. Q3Why do clubs churn off big team apps — price, complexity, parents refusing to install another app?
  4. Q4What breaks at the end of every season (rollover, re-registration, new teams) that someone dreads?
  5. Q5Who owns the spreadsheet, and what happens when that volunteer quits?
  6. Q6Which weekly task is done by someone who hates it — draws, rosters, chasing availability?
  7. Q7Which tasks have hard deadlines from a governing body (registrations, insurance, results submission)?
  8. Q8Can one sale to an association/region cover dozens of clubs instead of selling club by club?
  9. Q9In epic-to-story tools, who is the buyer — PM, BA, tech lead, or the dev who is stuck writing tickets?
  10. Q10Will individual PMs pay $10-20/month personally, as they already do for AI tools?
  11. Q11Will Atlassian/Linear built-in AI make epic-to-story generation a commodity — where is the defensible edge?
  12. Q12Where does AI add value that is not commodity — codebase context, domain templates, quality checking?
  13. Q13What is the one-person support burden — non-technical volunteers vs developers who self-serve?
  14. Q14Is seasonality a problem — does sport income only arrive in-season, and is that fine?
  15. Q15Could it be sold once (lifetime licence) rather than as SaaS to reduce churn anxiety?
  16. Q16Which marketplaces give free distribution — Atlassian, GitHub, Slack, Google Workspace, app stores?
  17. Q17How do you reach the first 100 paying customers without paid ads — which communities are reachable?
  18. Q18What data is locked inside the old tool, and can we import it in one click to remove switching pain?
  19. Q19How do you find poorly-maintained tools — stale marketplace ratings, last-updated dates, angry reviews?
  20. Q20What is the minimum price per club per season that makes the maths work for one person?
  21. Q21What compliance risk comes with it — children's data, payments, privacy law?
  22. Q22Can printable output (the PDF in PDFromScratch) be the hook — team sheets, draws, certificates, scorecards?
  23. Q23What is the 60-second aha demo for each idea?
  24. Q24Which idea lets us dogfood BMAD itself while building it?

Grand final

Convergence

Affinity Clustering then Impact-Effort. One hundred and one ideas went into the draw; this is what came out the other side.

The six pools

  1. 1

    No-app team coordination

    • availability via magic links/SMS/WhatsApp
    • duty rosters
    • carpools
    • coach-first spread

    Scout’s note high pain; crowded by free apps; support-heavy non-technical users

  2. 2

    Competition & tournament engine

    • constraint-based draws
    • rain-out rescheduler
    • one-day tournaments with live QR results
    • pay-per-event
    • venues/associations as buyers

    Scout’s note algorithmic moat, solo-friendly

  3. 3

    Legacy niche-software replacement

    • swim meets
    • Swiss chess pairing
    • athletics meets
    • ladders
    • court booking

    Scout’s note real incumbents are old Windows/PHP; domain-heavy, needs per-niche validation

  4. 4

    Repo-native / AI planning

    • markdown backlog in git
    • epic-to-story with BYO key
    • INVEST story linter
    • codebase-to-stories
    • CSV-to-tracker importer
    • marketplace apps

    Scout’s note developer buyers self-serve; commoditisation risk from Atlassian/GitHub AI

  5. 5

    Print-first PDF outputs

    • team sheets
    • scorecards
    • draws
    • certificates
    • quotes
    • compliance forms

    Scout’s note cross-cutting differentiator that matches the PDFromScratch name

  6. 6

    Go-to-market levers (not products)

    • validate with templates first
    • marketplace distribution
    • acquire an abandoned micro-SaaS
    • sell to associations not clubs

Impact × effort: the ladder

  1. 1

    Competition & tournament engine

    pay-per-event draws + rescheduler + print-ready PDFs

    high impact, medium effort

  2. 2

    Repo-native planning tool

    markdown backlog + AI epic-to-story + story linter

    medium-high impact, low-medium effort, dogfoods BMAD; must find an edge vs built-in tracker AI

  3. 3

    No-app availability + duty roster for grassroots clubs

    high impact but crowded and support-heavy; investigate only with a sharp wedge

  4. WC

    Replacing ancient Windows sport software (swim-meet management first)

    could be the best niche or a dead end; needs market research

Coach’s whiteboard · post-match

Five things we learned

  1. 1

    The two seed areas share one engine: hierarchical breakdown + constraint scheduling + printable output — season > rounds > fixtures > duties mirrors epic > story > task

    Sport season

    1. Season
    2. Rounds
    3. Fixtures
    4. Duties
    ⇄

    Software plan

    1. Epic
    2. Story
    3. Task
    breakdown + constraint scheduling + printable output
  2. 2

    Pay-per-event pricing dodges sport's seasonality and subscription resistance; venues and associations are the buyers with real budgets, clubs are the users

  3. 3

    Print-first is a real wedge: game day still runs on paper, and the PDFromScratch name already tells that story

  4. 4

    Cheapest validation path before code: sell a spreadsheet draw-generator / story-template kit, measure who buys, then build the SaaS for those buyers

  5. 5

    Dev-planning tools win on distribution (GitHub/Atlassian marketplaces) but lose on defensibility; sport tools are the reverse — hard to reach, but sticky once in

    DistributionDefensibility
    Dev-planning✓ win✗ lose
    Sport✗ hard to reach✓ sticky
