Distinct voices: about 6 association or official bodies (ZDH, Handwerkstag Sachsen, HDE/BDI/DIHK, BLTK, BDEW, Belgian and Polish SME bodies), 1 practitioner blogger plus a comment thread, at least 2 forum threads, and 2 developer threads. **Very few individual user posts were retrieved.** Most of the evidence is from associations, which suits readiness questions but is thin on direct customer voice.

## Stuck segments

1. **Handwerk (craft trades), especially construction and finishing trades.** Two thirds have problems receiving, only a third issue e-invoices, only a third archive per GoBD, and many plan to start only in H2 2027 (F1, F3). Construction specifications and service catalogues do not fit EN 16931, and the BMF says so itself (F8). Hybrid PDF and XML content can differ (F4). **Willingness to pay:** about €3k one-time plus recurring software costs per business (F2). That money flows to trade software makers (Handwerkersoftware), not to a library. The real bottleneck is the trade software vendors that implement EN 16931 "nicht korrekt" (F1).
2. **Small and legacy software vendors (Access, desktop, in-house tools).** Forum threads ask how to retrofit MS Access (F13). A practitioner had to write his own tool after his recipient rejected his invoice (F12). PHP library maintainers are thinly staffed (F15), and ZUGFeRD 2.5 forces another update (F17). **Willingness to pay:** indirect. Vendors pay for API engines such as Factora and DATEV connectors (F22), but no price points were found.
3. **Energy and utility billing (advance-payment plans, EDI@Energy).** These are format gaps with no clean mapping (F11). Buyers are few, specialised and well funded, but the space is dominated by specialist IT providers. No price signals were found.
4. **Medical, veterinary and therapy practices.** Mostly exempt from issuing, but they must receive, and side revenues and practice companies are in scope (F21). Their pain is awareness, not tooling. Willingness to pay is likely low.
5. **Vereine (associations).** Their business activities are in scope, but nothing was found showing they are stuck (F20). Willingness to pay is low.
6. **Users of Word/Excel invoices and freelancers.** Free generators already serve them (F19). Willingness to pay is near zero.

## Underserved spots

1. **Receiving-side "fix and explain" validation for real-world inbound invoices.** The pain is that two thirds of craft businesses struggle with receiving (F1), partners reject anything flagged invalid (F10), and the BMF says business-rule errors don't void an invoice's status (F8). A service could triage inbound files: KoSIT result, then a plain-language "does this actually matter for tax", plus a detector for mismatches between the PDF and the XML (F4). *Filters:* revenue is meaningful only when sold B2B2B to software vendors or tax firms, not to end firms. The market is partly crowded, since free viewers and validators exist (F18). It is buildable by 1–3 people. **Promising if sold to software vendors.**
2. **Embedded EN 16931 / ZUGFeRD 2.5 / XRechnung generation SDK or API for small trade software vendors.** The pain is vendors implementing EN 16931 incorrectly (F1), legacy stacks (F13), fragile open-source maintenance (F15) and the 2.5 update churn (F17). *Filters:* revenue is meaningful because it is per vendor, and they resell to thousands of end customers. It is moderately crowded (Factora, DATEV API, Mustang, commercial SDKs; F22). It is buildable. **Best fit**, especially if it adds trade-specific templates (construction specification and partial-invoice mapping, F8) that general engines lack.
3. **Construction and trade invoice mapping profile (specification positions, partial and final invoices, retentions) as a library add-on.** This is an officially acknowledged gap (F8) in the segment with the worst readiness (F1, F3). *Filters:* revenue per customer is meaningful only through trade software vendors. It is not crowded as far as we can see. It is buildable, but depends on rules still in flux (EU mapping due H2 2026, F4). **Promising but carries regulatory risk.**
4. **One engine for several countries: DE (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD) + FR (Factur-X, Plateforme Agréée flows) + BE (Peppol BIS).** Belgium's problems "lie with the software developers" (F24). France receives since Sep 2026 and SMEs issue from Sep 2027 (F25). Factur-X is the same standard family as ZUGFeRD, and Mustang is already moving there (F27). *Filters:* revenue per customer is meaningful for software vendors selling in several countries. Peppol access points are crowded and French certification is heavy, so the pure format layer is the 1–3 person sweet spot. Buildable only if it skips becoming a Plateforme Agréée or a Peppol access point. **Promising as a format-only layer.**
5. **GoBD-compliant archive plus audit trail for small issuers and receivers.** Only a third archive compliantly (F1, F3). *Filters:* revenue per customer is small for end users. DMS and DATEV crowd the space. **Weak.**
6. **Poland KSeF.** This is a clearance model, structurally different from EN 16931, so a DE-first tool cannot easily serve it (F26). **Out of scope.**

## Looked for but not found

Advance payments appear only through the energy-sector submission (F11) and ETL guidance on not reissuing earlier partial invoices.

- **Direct user complaints from Vereine, medical or therapy practices.** Only advisory content was found.
- **Stack Overflow threads from 2025–26.** None surfaced.
- **Pricing or willingness-to-pay data for API or SDK products.** None found, apart from the ZDH's €3k one-time implementation cost per craft business.
