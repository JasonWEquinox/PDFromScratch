# Customer voice and unmet needs: Atlassian DC EOL, plus Zendesk/monday gaps (round 1, lens 1)

Run date: 2026-11-22. Method: 18 WebSearch/WebFetch calls in standard mode. WebFetch was blocked: community.atlassian.com and news.ycombinator.com did not resolve in this session. reddit.com is blocked for search. **Every claim below therefore rests on search-result snippets, and confidence is capped at Medium.** I could not open thread bodies, so the distinct-voice counts are lower bounds taken from snippets. Usernames are redacted.

**Freshness warning:** the densest customer-voice source, the community thread "Good-Bye Data Center", started in Sept 2025. That is about 14 months before this run, which is outside the 12-month sentiment window. Fresher items (Mar–Nov 2026) are mostly press, vendor and Atlassian pages, not posts by real users.

## Top blockers / requests (ranked)

Voice counts are distinct people or outlets visible in snippets. Many are lower bounds because I could not open the threads.

1. **Missing or non-equivalent Cloud apps, and scripted customisations that don't migrate** (ScriptRunner REST endpoints, mail handlers, conditions, script fields). Voices: 1 community author (F7) + 2 or more users on SR threads (F11) + "not possible in Cloud" user (F5) + legacy-integration user (F4) = **≥5**. Refs: F4, F5, F7, F11.
2. **Air-gapped, government and defence environments** (SCADA, FedRAMP High/IL5 not yet authorised, air-gap "in design"). Voices: 1 SCADA user (F4) + The Register (F8) + 2 vendor commentators (F12, F13) = **≥4**, of which only 1 is a real user. Refs: F2, F4, F8, F12, F13.
3. **Cost and TCO uplift** (about 28% more on average; DC price rises of 15–25% in Feb 2026; Cloud price revision in Oct 2026; paid add-ons such as Guard). Voices: 1 consultant via The Register (F8) + "invested heavily" user (F3) = **≥2**, plus Atlassian pricing documents. Refs: F3, F8, F14.
4. **Trust and broken promise, leading to evaluation of alternatives** (Docmost, Plane, GitLab, Inflectra; requests to open-source DC). Voices: **≥3** users (F3, F5, F6). Refs: F3, F5, F6.
5. **EU data residency and sovereignty.** Voices: ≥1 in 2025 (F5). The other evidence is from 2020. Weak and stale. Ref: F5, F16.
6. **Migration tooling speed and reliability at large scale** (TB-scale attachments, slow JCMA, JCMA reporting false success for apps). Voices: Register (F9) + ≥2 users (F10, F11). Refs: F9, F10, F11.

Zendesk/monday:
- monday: **native cross-board or portfolio view and "core" gaps pushed to paid apps.** ≥3 voices (F21, F22). This is the clearest recurring theme.
- Zendesk: requests are long-tail and narrow (Talk localisation, scoping Procedures by form or group, per-brand language). I found no single dominant gap. ≥3 requests seen, but I could not count distinct voices.

## Underserved spots

1. **DC-to-Cloud "custom logic" migration and parity tooling** (ScriptRunner, Groovy and mail-handler conversion to Forge/Automation, with a verification report showing what JCMA silently dropped). Evidence: F7, F11, F9. Filters: *Revenue per customer* is high, because enterprise migrations are paid projects and services-attached pricing is plausible. *Crowding* is medium: Adaptavist's own ScriptRunner Connect and migration suite are incumbents (F11), so a solo player would need a narrow wedge such as an "audit what JCMA didn't migrate" diff report. *1–3 people* is feasible for an analysis or report tool, and hard for a full converter. The window closes after 2029, so this is a time-boxed business.
2. **Post-EOL read-only DC archive / "keep the lights on" viewer** for teams that have to keep records (regulated, A&D, SCADA) but won't migrate. Evidence: F1 (instances go read-only), F4, F6, F13. Filters: *Revenue* is high per customer (compliance budgets). *Crowding* is low: I saw no product in this run. *1–3 people* is feasible, as a static export plus searchable archive of Jira/Confluence data. The risks are Atlassian's licence terms and whether buyers will purchase from a tiny vendor. Confidence is Low-Medium.
3. **Exit to self-hosted alternatives for air-gapped and government shops** (DC to Plane, OpenProject, GitLab, Docmost importers with fidelity on workflows, custom fields and links). Evidence: F4, F5, F12, F13. Filters: *Revenue* is medium-high. *Crowding* is rising, because the alternative vendors are building their own importers (F12, F13). *1–3 people* is feasible for a Confluence-to-X fidelity converter. Distribution outside the Atlassian Marketplace is the hard part.
4. **Cloud cost and licence-tier optimiser for migrators** (identify inactive users, model Standard/Premium/Enterprise plus Guard costs before and after the move). Evidence: F8, F14. Filters: *Revenue* is medium. *Crowding* is likely moderate: I did not verify existing licence-optimiser apps in this run. *1–3 people* is feasible.
5. **monday.com cross-board portfolio / holistic project view.** Evidence: F21, F22. Traction signals: F19, F20. Filters: *Revenue per customer* is low to medium, because monday seats are SMB-heavy. *Crowding* is likely high: portfolio and dashboard apps are common, though I did not verify that in this run. *1–3 people* is feasible, and monday's tiering and case studies show small vendors can reach $30K MRR (vendor-curated). The weakest fit to the "not small, not crowded" filters.
6. **Zendesk narrow admin gaps** (Procedures scoped by form or group, per-brand end-user language, Talk callback localisation). Evidence: F17, F18. Filters: *Revenue* is unclear because I found no earnings data. *Crowding* is unknown. *1–3 people* is feasible. The evidence is too thin to rank this above the Atlassian spots.
