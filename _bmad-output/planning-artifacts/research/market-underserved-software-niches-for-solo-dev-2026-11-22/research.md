# Market research: underserved software niches for a solo dev or small team (2026)

**Decision this research serves:** which 3–5 market-gap areas deserve a deeper look for a 1–3 person product business.

**Hard filters applied:** (1) not small or boring, meaning meaningful revenue per customer; (2) not crowded or commodity; (3) buildable and runnable by 1–3 people.

> **Evidence limits, read first.** This session's network blocked Reddit, Indie Hackers, the QuickBooks community, EU Commission pages and several data sites. Most claims rest on search-result snippets, not full pages. Load-bearing claims were cross-checked against an independent source (see the ledger). Everything else carries one source, with its confidence marked. Round 2 was skipped for the same reason; see Open questions.

## Executive summary

**What the evidence says to do:** look deeper at gaps created by a **forced, dated change**: a shutdown, end of life, mandate, or price shock. Sell to **business buyers who aren't developers**, at a price above roughly $100–250 a month, ideally **through an established B2B marketplace**.

Three findings drive this:

1. **Forced changes create dated, unavoidable demand.** Three were confirmed this run:
   - Shopify shut down its free Stocky purchase-order app on 31 Aug 2026 [1][2].
   - Atlassian Data Center reaches end of life on 28 Mar 2029, and new-customer sales ended in March 2026 [3][4].
   - Germany makes B2B e-invoice *issuance* mandatory from 1 Jan 2027 (turnover above €800k) and from 1 Jan 2028 for all businesses [9][10].

   In each case people *must* act, and the incumbent or platform answer leaves part of the market unserved.
2. **Solo founders do make real money on mid-size B2B marketplaces.** A solo Shopify app reports about $125–142K MRR [11], and a solo Zendesk app business reports about $51K a month [12]. Shopify itself is crowded (median price $9.99, and most apps have no reviews) [14], so the less crowded B2B marketplaces look better.
3. **When a tool annoys developers, the demand goes to free tools.** After Postman's free-plan restriction [15] and Heroku's feature freeze [17], open-source tools and funded platforms absorbed the switchers within weeks. Cheap AI tools keep customers badly: roughly 23% of revenue retained after a year under $50/month (secondary source) [13]. Price and audience matter more than how loud the anger is.

**Biggest caveat:** the evidence is snippet-level and short on user voice (no Reddit). Every ranked gap below needs a quick round of customer conversations or a demand test before you commit.

## Dimension 1: stale or overpriced incumbents

- **Atlassian Data Center end of life.** Jira, JSM and Confluence DC go read-only after 28 Mar 2029. New-customer sales ended 30 Mar 2026, and existing customers can buy until 30 Mar 2028 [3][4]. Atlassian concedes that some regulated organisations need another deployment option, and GitLab and OpenText are courting them [3][4]. *Open space:* migration, archiving, and DC-only Marketplace apps losing their channel. The head-on replacement market is contested.
- **QuickBooks Desktop price anger.** It has lasted through 2025–26: users report increases of 38–59% a year, and one user's costs went from $1,106 (2021) to $4,149 (2026). Intuit's Canadian FAQ confirms another increase from October 2026 [16]. *Open space:* bridges and migration for bookkeepers. A full general-ledger replacement is crowded and carries compliance burden. (Unverified, medium.)
- **Microsoft Publisher removed from Microsoft 365 on 1 Oct 2026** [5][6]. Users ask for a replacement, and Microsoft points to Word, PowerPoint and Designer. Demand peaks now and fades, and revenue per customer is low. **Parked.**
- **Mindbody** (studio software): long-running pricing and lock-in complaints, but many funded challengers. **Crowded.**
- **Acquisition marketplaces:** the tiniest projects sell for about $3–6K [19]. Products with about $200K revenue ask 2.5–4x revenue, and buyers pay a premium for retention [20]. That favours sticky B2B workflow products over one-off utilities.

## Dimension 2: pain users describe themselves

- **Stocky shutdown** (strongest user voice): at least four Shopify Community merchant threads plus the developer forum. Merchants say the native purchase-order feature is "brutally inadequate" and missing four-decimal cost precision. ERP-level replacements are "overkill", so merchants fall back to Google Sheets, and indie replacements are already launching [1][2]. Confidence: high on the event, medium on the size of the market.
- **Landlords with 20–100 units** pay $40–$907 a year for property-management software, yet build lender-grade rent rolls and trailing-12 reports by hand [18]. This comes from one community only. (Unverified.)
- **Accounting firms chasing client documents** (AccountingWEB, Intuit ProSeries, surveys). **Crowded** (TaxDome, Financial Cents, Content Snare). It could only work as a jurisdiction-specific wedge, such as UK Making Tax Digital, which is unverified.
- **Tracking contractors' insurance certificates (COIs) for small landlords and builders:** spreadsheets go stale. Partly crowded (myCOI, TrustLayer).
- **Caution:** "I'd pay for this" rarely converts, and $5/month is a bad price point [21]. People pay for tools that bring in revenue, carry liability, or that they use weekly [21].

## Dimension 3: newly created gaps

- **Germany e-invoicing:** issuance becomes mandatory from 1 Jan 2027 (prior-year turnover above €800k) and from 1 Jan 2028 for everyone. Receipt has been mandatory since 2025 [9][10]. A Finance Ministry (BMF) letter dated 15 Oct 2026 concedes gaps in the formats [9]. Germany requires no certified platform. The long tail of vertical and legacy software (trades, practices, associations) looks underserved. The formats (ZUGFeRD and Factur-X) are **hybrid PDF+XML files**.
- **Poland KSeF:** the mandate is phasing in, and the smallest firms join from 1 Jan 2027. The **penalty start was pushed back to 31 Dec 2027**, pending legislation [7][8]. A free government tool exists, and Polish-language market access is a barrier. **Demoted.**
- **France:** SMEs must issue e-invoices and e-report by 1 Sep 2027, through roughly 150 certified platforms, more than ten of them free. Basic issuance is commoditised. Only connectors for vertical software remain open.
- **California automated-decision rules:** they apply from 1 Jan 2027, and risk assessments are due 1 Apr 2028. There may be a mid-market gap below enterprise privacy platforms such as OneTrust, but there is legal-liability risk. (Unverified.)
- **Rejected as crowded or low urgency:** EU AI Act high-risk readiness (pushed back to Dec 2027), PCI payment-page script monitoring, AI-search visibility, LLM cost monitoring, and one-off API-sunset migrations.

## Dimension 4: proof points

- **Add-ons on mid-size B2B platform marketplaces** have the strongest evidence for solo income [11][12]. Marketplace fees are favourable (Shopify takes 0% on the first $1M lifetime; Atlassian takes 5% in year one). The main risk is the platform building the feature itself: one founder lost about $2K MRR when Zendesk shipped a native feature [12].
- **Per-unit back-office tools** for agencies and MSPs (per site, per client account) multiply revenue per customer.
- **Vertical SaaS for one trade or regulated service** is plausible, but this run found no fresh evidence for it.
- **Saturated:** thin AI wrappers [13], generic Shopify conversion apps [14], no-code builders, and generic micro-SaaS.

## Cross-dimension insights

1. **Forced change × marketplace distribution × a business buyer is the shape that passes all three filters.** Forced change provides urgency (dimensions 1 and 3), the marketplace provides solo-sized distribution (dimension 4), and a non-developer buyer avoids free-tool absorption (dimensions 1 and 2).
2. **Platform features cut both ways.** The same platforms that distribute solo apps also kill them, by shutting down (Stocky) or by shipping a native feature (Zendesk). The best gaps are ones where the platform has signalled it *won't* fill the gap: Shopify said Stocky-parity features aren't coming in 2026 [2], and Atlassian is removing Data Center.
3. **Compliance plus PDF.** Germany's e-invoice formats are PDF files with embedded XML. A "PDF from scratch" competence is unusually on-point for that gap. It's a happy accident of the repo name, but a real technical edge.

**Parked:** Microsoft Publisher replacement (urgency peaked, low revenue), Poland KSeF (penalties deferred, language barrier), California automated-decision rules (liability), EU AI Act (deferred), studio management (crowded), document chasing for accounting firms (crowded), and anything aimed at developers with free open-source alternatives.

## Recommendations

1. **Take gaps #1 and #2 into the Creative Partner brainstorm** as the main candidates, with #3 as a quick-test option. Feeds: the product brief's problem and opportunity sections. *Basis:* the deadlines and events behind them were verified, but crowding is unverified, so the confidence is medium.
2. **Before committing, run a focused competitive pass (`bmad-deep-recon`, competitive type) on the chosen gap.** For #1, that means existing e-invoice libraries and APIs and their prices. For #2, it means Atlassian Marketplace migration and archiving apps. This closes the biggest unknown: whether the space is really uncrowded.
3. **Validate demand cheaply** (landing page, marketplace listing waitlist, or 5–10 buyer conversations) before building a minimum viable product. *Basis:* stated willingness to pay is unreliable [21].

## Open questions

- **Crowding in each gap:** competitor lists and prices were not gathered for #1, #2 or #4. A competitive Deep Recon run would answer this.
- **User voice from Reddit, Indie Hackers and MSP communities:** blocked in this session. Answerable by allowing those hosts in the environment's network settings, or by drafting a prompt for your own deep-research tool (Deep Recon *Draft* mode).
- **Size of the market for Stocky refugees:** how many merchants used Stocky, and how many have already moved.
- **Whether any domain edge applies:** language, regional market access (Germany and the EU vs NZ/AU), or existing contacts in any of these buyer groups.
