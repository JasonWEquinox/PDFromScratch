# Digest: Stale or overpriced incumbents (round 1, researcher 1)

Dimension: stale, abandoned or overpriced incumbents; "X alternative" demand; micro-SaaS marketplace signals.
Run date: 2026-11-22. 19 tool calls: 15 web searches and 4 page fetches. All 4 fetches failed because the session network blocks these hosts: bigideasdb.com, paddle.com, quickbooks.intuit.com and newsletter.microns.io. So the evidence below comes mostly from search-result snippets. Confidence is lowered where only a snippet or a competitor's page backs a claim.

## Candidate gaps

G1. **A replacement for Microsoft Publisher aimed at newsletters, bulletins and print layouts, with faithful .pub import and conversion.**
- Who pays: churches, schools, clubs, small print shops and local nonprofits with years of .pub templates, plus IT departments handling the migration (one-off licence, or about $5–15/month prosumer).
- Evidence: F8, F13.
- Not small or boring? Borderline. Revenue per customer is modest, but a forced, dated migration creates real urgency, and buyers include institutions.
- Not crowded? Partly. Affinity, Scribus, LibreOffice Draw and Canva exist, but F8 says LibreOffice handles .pub imperfectly. High-fidelity .pub conversion plus newsletter templates looks like an open wedge (unverified).
- Buildable by 1–3 people? Yes: a desktop or web app and file-format work, with no regulation.
- Caveat: the shutdown has already happened (October 2026), so demand peaks now and then falls.

G2. **Tools for organisations staying on Atlassian Data Center and for those leaving it.** Examples: Jira/Confluence export and migration utilities for self-hosted targets, compliance archiving, or a lightweight self-hosted Confluence or Jira-Service-Desk replacement for regulated mid-size organisations.
- Who pays: IT and engineering teams in regulated or sovereignty-sensitive organisations, and Marketplace app vendors who are losing the Data Center channel.
- Evidence: F1, F2.
- Not small or boring? Yes: high revenue per customer and a hard deadline (no new licences now; end of life in March 2029).
- Not crowded? Head-on replacement is contested by GitLab and OpenText. Migration tooling for niche apps and archiving looks less covered (unverified).
- Buildable by 1–3 people? Utilities, yes. A full replacement carries a risk of enterprise sales cycles.

G3. **Companion tools or a migration path for small businesses priced out of QuickBooks Desktop and unwilling to move to QBO.** For example, a "stay on old perpetual Desktop" toolkit: payroll and bank-feed bridges, a QB Desktop-to-Xero migration service, or industry-specific job-costing add-ons.
- Who pays: small businesses and their bookkeepers and accountants, who are already spending $1K–4K+ a year (F3).
- Evidence: F3, F4, F5.
- Not small or boring? Yes: meaningful spend and anger that has lasted years.
- Not crowded? A head-on general-ledger replacement is crowded (Xero, Wave, Zoho). Bridges and migration tooling are less crowded.
- Buildable by 1–3 people? Bridges and migration, yes. A full accounting or payroll product brings tax and compliance load, which fails the filter.

G4. **Studio-management software for one specific modality (for example Pilates, yoga, martial arts or dance), positioned against Mindbody's pricing and lock-in, with free data export as the hook.**
- Who pays: independent studio owners, at roughly $100–300/month.
- Evidence: F9.
- Not small or boring? Yes: SMB vertical SaaS with good revenue per customer.
- Not crowded? This is the weak point. Many funded challengers exist (Glofox, PushPress, WellnessLiving, Vibefam, Momence), so a solo entrant needs a very narrow vertical.
- Buildable by 1–3 people? Yes, though payments integrations add work.

G5. **Self-hosted SMB virtualisation management and support tooling (Proxmox-centric): backup orchestration, migration-from-VMware wizards, fleet dashboards for MSPs.**
- Who pays: MSPs and SMB IT departments leaving VMware.
- Evidence: F11.
- Not small or boring? Yes: high revenue per customer, and MSPs pay for productivity.
- Not crowded? Proxmox's own tools and Veeam cover part of it. The fit for MSP-oriented multi-cluster tooling needs checking.
- Buildable by 1–3 people? Yes. Pure software, though it requires infrastructure expertise.

G6. **Low-code internal-database products for FileMaker shops that find Claris platform licensing too costly.** Either a FileMaker-file-compatible web runtime or a migration tool to a cheaper stack.
- Who pays: SMBs with long-lived FileMaker solutions, and the independent FileMaker developer channel.
- Evidence: F12.
- Not small or boring? Yes: B2B with high switching value.
- Not crowded? Many low-code tools exist (Airtable, Knack and others), but few offer FileMaker migration specifically (unverified).
- Buildable by 1–3 people? Migration tooling, yes. Evidence of pain is weak (low confidence).

G7. **Not a gap, a negative lesson: API clients (Postman) and PaaS (Heroku).** Anger is high and visible (F6, F7), but free/open-source tools (Bruno, Insomnia) and well-funded platforms (Render, Fly.io, Railway) absorbed the demand within weeks. Visible "X alternative" content volume is not a good proxy for openness when the switchers are developers who will choose free tools.

**Marketplace implication (F14–F17).** Micro projects (Microns, about $3–6K per deal) sell at hobby prices. Real value builds in the roughly $200K-revenue band on Acquire, at about 2.5–4x revenue. Buyers pay for retention (net revenue retention above 110%, gross retention above 90%). That favours sticky B2B workflow and migration-locked products, such as G2, G3 and G5, over one-off utilities, such as G1.
